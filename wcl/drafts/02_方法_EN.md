## III. Proposed Method

### A. Hierarchical Multi-Agent DRL Framework

We adopt a hierarchical structure with two decision layers that match the
different time scales of the problem.

*Upper layer (trajectory and scheduling).* Each UAV is controlled by a
continuous policy that outputs, per slot, its movement (displacement and
speed) and a binary scheduling decision indicating whether it forwards buffered
data to the RBS. The upper policies are trained with multi-agent
deep-deterministic policy gradient (MADDPG) under the centralized-training /
decentralized-execution (CTDE) paradigm, where each actor observes only its own
local state and the centralized critic has access to the joint state and
actions.

*Lower layer (access control and mode selection).* For each GU, a discrete
policy selects the access decision (served or not) and the transmission mode
(active RF or backscatter). The lower controllers are independent DQNs, one per
GU, trained with experience replay. The lower-layer reward reflects the data
received by the serving UAV minus a term penalizing the GU energy consumed from
its harvested budget.

The two layers are coupled through the environment: the upper-layer decisions
determine coverage and scheduling, which in turn shape the lower-layer
interactions, while the lower-layer decisions determine how much data is
available for the upper layer to forward.

### B. Local World Model for the Lower Layer

The key observation is that the lower-layer state transitions — the GU buffer
occupancy and the GU harvested/consumed energy — follow the closed-form
physical equations in Section II-C. We therefore train a compact feed-forward
world model $\mathcal{W}_\theta$ that predicts, for a lower-layer state-action
pair $(s, a)$, the next state $s'$ and the reward $r$:

$$ (r, s') = \mathcal{W}_\theta(s, a), \quad (6)$$

supervised by the *real* replay transitions collected during training
(mean-squared-error on both $r$ and $s'$). The model is deliberately small
(~8.5k parameters, [TBD: verify against code]) and does not use black-box
latent prediction; its inputs/outputs are interpretable buffer and energy
quantities governed by the physical model.

### C. Dyna-Q Virtual Planning for the Lower Layer Only

We integrate the learned world model into the lower-layer DQNs following the
Dyna-Q paradigm. At regular training steps, the model generates virtual
transitions from real or sampled states, and these virtual transitions are used
to perform additional Q-learning updates for the lower DQNs — reducing the
number of real environment interactions needed to reach a target performance
level.

Two design choices are deliberate:

1. **Planning is restricted to the lower layer.** The upper-layer continuous
   trajectory transitions lack a reliable closed-form model; predicting them in
   continuous space would amplify model error. The lower-layer discrete
   decisions, by contrast, are governed by the well-defined buffer and energy
   dynamics, for which the world model is accurate. Restricting planning to
   this reliably-modeled subspace limits the risk of model-error accumulation.
2. **Planning starts only after a warm-up phase.** No virtual planning is
   performed until the world model has been trained on a sufficient number of
   real transitions (warm-up of 128 episodes, chosen by an ablation). This
   prevents the lower DQN from being updated by an immature model.

The planning depth is set to $K=1$ virtual update per real update; a formal
paired ablation showed that larger $K$ does not provide a stable benefit while
roughly doubling the wall-clock cost.

### D. Training Procedure

```
Algorithm 1: Hierarchical DRL with lower-layer Dyna-Q planning
 1: Initialize upper actors/critics (MADDPG), lower DQNs, world model W_θ
 2: for episode = 1, 2, ... do
 3:   reset environment; observe states
 4:   repeat
 5:     upper action ← upper actors; lower action ← lower DQNs
 6:     execute full action; observe (r, s'), step_info
 7:     store real transition in replay buffers
 8:     update upper actors/critics (MADDPG)
 9:     update lower DQNs from real replay
10:     if world model warm-up done then
11:       update W_θ on real transitions        // supervised by physical eqs.
12:       draw K=1 virtual transitions from W_θ; update lower DQNs on them
13:     end if
14:   until slot done
15: end for
```

Virtual transitions are used only to update the lower-layer DQNs during
training; they never affect the real environment execution.
