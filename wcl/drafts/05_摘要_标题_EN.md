## 摘要（约 180–200 词）

**Abstract** — We consider the joint trajectory planning, transmission
scheduling, and access control problem in UAV-assisted wireless sensor
networks, where the lower-layer discrete controller of a hierarchical
multi-agent deep reinforcement learning (MARL) framework converges slowly and
requires many real environment interactions. We augment the lower layer with a
lightweight local world model supervised by the closed-form buffer and
energy-harvesting dynamics, and apply Dyna-Q virtual planning exclusively to
the reliably-modeled lower-layer decisions, with planning depth $K=1$ after a
128-episode model warm-up. In a five-seed evaluation against a hierarchical
model-free baseline with identical network structure, the proposed method
reaches 90% of its peak performance in approximately 1.6× fewer real
interactions, while attaining essentially the same final system energy
efficiency (delivered-to-RBS data per unit of UAV energy). A paired ablation
shows that a deeper planning depth provides no reproducible benefit while
roughly doubling the wall-clock cost. These results indicate that a
physically-consistent local world model is an effective way to reduce the
real-environment interaction cost of hierarchical DRL in UAV-assisted sensing
networks, without degrading final performance.
