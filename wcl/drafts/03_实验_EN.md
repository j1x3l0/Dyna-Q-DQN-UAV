## IV. Experiments

### A. Setup

We evaluate the proposed method on a UAV-assisted WSN with $N=3$ UAVs and
$M=6$ energy-harvesting GUs deployed over a $1\,\text{km}\times1\,\text{km}$
area (Case 1: random initial positions). The UAV flies at $50$–$100$ m
altitude with $v_{max}=25$ m/s; the Rician factor is $K=10$. The reward is the
paper-aligned objective $\Xi$ (delivered-to-RBS data per unit of UAV energy),
which we verified to be highly correlated with the scalar reward
(correlation ≈ 1.0). We compare five algorithms: the single-layer MADDPG
baseline, the hierarchical model-free controller (NoDyna), the proposed
hierarchical Dyna-Q controller, and two extended multi-agent baselines (MATD3
and CoP-MADDPG, hyperparameters selected by a short gate). All algorithms share
the same environment, reward, early-stopping rule (patience 1500 episodes,
window 500), and 5 seeds $\{42,123,2026,3407,8888\}$.

**Findings.** (i) The hierarchical controllers (NoDyna and Dyna-Q) achieve a
substantially higher final $\Xi$ than the single-layer MADDPG baseline
($\approx 0.017$ versus $0.0098$), consistent with the benefit of decoupling
the two decision scales. (ii) Dyna-Q and NoDyna attain essentially the same
final $\Xi$ ($0.0171$ vs $0.0172$): the learned model does **not** sacrifice
final performance. Both statements are descriptive trends from 5 paired seeds;
they do not reach statistical significance (paired exact tests, Holm-corrected).

### C. Sample Efficiency (Core Claim)

Fig. 2 shows the episode reward against the number of *real* environment
interactions. Dyna-Q reaches 90% of its peak performance in $330 \pm 169$
episodes versus $525 \pm 301$ for NoDyna — a **1.59×** descriptive reduction in
real interactions, without a final-performance loss. This trend is directional
and not statistically significant (Holm-corrected $p=0.3125$; with 5 seeds the
minimum attainable exact $p$ is 0.0625). We therefore claim that model-driven
planning *tends to* reduce real interactions, and present this as the main
result rather than a final-performance improvement.

### D. Ablations

*Planning depth K.* A paired 5-seed ablation ($K=1$ vs $K=5$, warm-up 32) showed
no reproducible benefit of deeper planning: $K=5$ raised the final $\Xi$ by
only $6.3\%$ (paired exact $p=0.875$, bootstrap 95% CI spanning zero) while
increasing the wall-clock time by $105.7\%$ and delaying the retrospective 90%
peak by $\approx 94\%$. We therefore fix $K=1$.

*Model warm-up.* A single-seed engineering gate over warm-up
$\in\{0,32,64,128\}$ selected 128 episodes: it reduced the final world-model
loss by $\approx 86\%$ relative to 32 while improving early reward AUC. This is
an engineering choice, not a statistical claim.

### E. Discussion: Sample Efficiency vs Wall-Clock

Dyna-Q's reduction in real interactions comes at a *longer* wall-clock time
($17.5$ h vs $11.5$ h for NoDyna), because virtual planning adds GPU computation.
Sample efficiency (real interactions) and compute efficiency (wall-clock) are
therefore distinct. In a deployed UAV system, each real interaction involves
physical flight and channel measurement whose cost dominates GPU computation;
the reduction in real interactions is what matters for deployment.
