## V. Conclusion

We considered the joint trajectory planning, transmission scheduling, and
access control problem in UAV-assisted wireless sensor networks under a
hierarchical multi-agent reinforcement learning framework. To address the slow
convergence of the lower-layer discrete controller — a bottleneck explicitly
identified by the original hierarchical MARL work — we augmented the
lower layer with a lightweight local world model supervised by the closed-form
buffer and energy-harvesting dynamics, and applied Dyna-Q virtual planning
exclusively to the reliably-modeled lower-layer decisions ($K=1$, after a
128-episode warm-up).

Across 5 paired seeds, the proposed model-driven controller reaches 90% of its
peak performance in roughly 1.6× fewer real environment interactions than the
model-free hierarchical baseline, while attaining essentially the same final
system energy efficiency. The reduction in real interactions is directional and
not statistically significant at 5 seeds; we present it as the primary claim.
Ablations show that a deeper planning depth ($K=5$) brings no reproducible
benefit while roughly doubling wall-clock cost, and that the planning depth and
warm-up are robust engineering choices.

We conclude that integrating a physically-consistent local world model is a
promising way to reduce the real-environment interaction cost of hierarchical
DRL in UAV-assisted sensing networks, without degrading final performance.

### Limitations

- **Scenario scope.** The method targets open-field deployments (UAV altitude
  50–100 m, where ground obstacles are not an operative constraint and a strong
  LoS Rician channel is appropriate). UAV-to-UAV collision avoidance and
  airspace geofencing are modeled; urban occlusion, low-altitude obstacle
  avoidance, and no-fly zones are outside the scope of this work.
- **Simplified dynamics.** Slotted kinematics with speed and altitude limits;
  no full flight-control loop, acceleration, or wind.
- **Analytic channel.** Rician fading is resampled i.i.d. per slot; spatial
  correlation and building blockage are not modeled. Ray-tracing validation is
  left to future work.
- **No hardware validation.** Results are simulation evidence under controlled
  conditions; delays, sensing noise, and flight-execution error are not
  included.
- **Limited scale.** Results are reported for $N=3$, $M=6$, and a single
  deployment scenario; other scales and initial deployments are future work.
- **Collision penalty ≠ safety guarantee.** The soft/hard penalties reduce
  collisions but do not constitute a formal safety certificate.
- **Energy accounting.** UAV energy is used as the denominator of the efficiency
  metric; the paper does not claim battery feasibility.

### Future Work

Natural extensions include: robustness across initial deployments (Case 2),
model-confidence-adaptive planning (choosing $K$ and whether to plan based on
world-model uncertainty), ray-traced channel validation in urban scenarios
(e.g., via Sionna), and larger system scales.
